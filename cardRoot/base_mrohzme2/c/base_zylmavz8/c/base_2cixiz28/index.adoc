A controlled document (`base/cardTypes/controlledDocument`) is a formal document with an approval workflow. It has an owner, an approver, an approval date, an information classification, and a review period in days. Its workflow states are Draft, Approved, and Archived.

The template "Controlled document" makes a document and one review task ("Document review", `base/cardTypes/reviewTask`) under it. The template "Process description" makes a controlled document for a process. To add more review tasks, use the button "Add a new review task" in the document, or the template "Document review".

== Approve a document

"Approve" is denied until all of these are true:

* The document has a review task.
* All its review tasks are closed as done.
* At least one of its review tasks has no policy check failures.
* The owner, the approver, and the information classification are set.
* The document has no policy check failures.

"Approve" sets the approval date to the current date. An approved or archived document cannot be edited. To change it, reopen it. "Reopen" sets it back to Draft.

== Review tasks

The owner of a review task is the reviewer. A review task needs an owner before "Start progress" and "Done". The reviewer must not be the owner of the document. When they are the same person, both cards fail the policy check "Reviewer cannot be the same as document owner".

The transitions that close a review task set its "Last reviewed" date to the current date.

== What the rules check

A document that is not a draft fails a policy check when its owner, approver, approval date, or review period is missing.

An approved document also fails a policy check in these cases:

* It has no review task: "Review task missing".
* One of its review tasks is not closed as done: "The review subtasks of approved documents must be closed".
* All its review tasks fail a policy check: "Failing policy checks in all review tasks".
* All its closed reviews are older than the review period: "Review has expired". Complete a new review to fix it.
